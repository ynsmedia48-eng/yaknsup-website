The Best Day Trip from Phoenix or Scottsdale Is One Most People Haven't Taken

By Yak N Sup·June 2026·7 min read·Canyon Lake, AZ

People visiting Phoenix and Scottsdale tend to do the same things: Old Town Scottsdale, Camelback Mountain, Sedona if they've got a full day, maybe a hot air balloon. All great. But there's a day trip that most visitors completely miss — one that's closer than Sedona, requires no serious hiking, and delivers scenery that genuinely shocks people who thought they knew what Arizona looked like.

Canyon Lake and the Apache Trail. Forty-five minutes east of Scottsdale, inside the Tonto National Forest, at the base of the Superstition Mountains. Towering canyon walls. Crystal-clear desert water. Bighorn sheep on the cliffs. A 100-year-old mining town serving chili and pie on a dirt road with no cell service.

It's the Arizona that doesn't make the Instagram highlight reels — and it's better for it.

How to Get There

From Scottsdale or the east Valley: take US-60 east toward Apache Junction, then head north on AZ-88 (the Apache Trail). Canyon Lake Marina is about 16 miles up the Apache Trail — roughly 40–50 minutes from Scottsdale depending on your starting point.

From central Phoenix: add about 10–15 minutes. From Tempe or Mesa: similar to Scottsdale.

🗺️ The Drive Is Part of the Trip

AZ-88 — the Apache Trail — is one of the most scenic roads in Arizona. It follows the route of the original construction road used to build the Salt River dams in the early 1900s. The road climbs through desert foothills, past Goldfield Ghost Town, then cuts along cliff edges above the Salt River chain of lakes. Leave early enough to enjoy it. There's no reason to rush this drive.

The Perfect One-Day Itinerary

1
8:00 – 8:30 AM · Departure

Leave Phoenix / Scottsdale Early

Early morning is the best time to be at Canyon Lake — calmer water, better light, more wildlife activity, and cooler temperatures in summer. Aim to arrive at Canyon Lake Marina by 9–9:30 AM. Grab coffee before you leave; there's nothing on AZ-88.

2
Optional Stop · Apache Junction

Goldfield Ghost Town

On AZ-88 just before the Tonto National Forest boundary, Goldfield Ghost Town is a restored 1890s mining settlement with a small museum, mine tours, and a narrow-gauge railroad. Opens at 10am. Worth a 20–30 minute stop if you're not in a rush to get to the lake.

3
9:30 AM – 12:00 PM · Canyon Lake Marina

Kayak or Paddleboard with Yak N Sup

This is the centerpiece of the day. Book a 1-hour or 2-hour rental — or a guided Canyon Tour if you want someone to take you to the best spots (La Barge Creek, the hidden coves, areas most self-guided paddlers miss). No experience needed. Life jackets and paddles included. The canyon walls from the water are unlike anything you'll see from the road.

4
12:30 – 2:00 PM · 5 Miles East on AZ-88

Lunch at Tortilla Flat

Tortilla Flat is a legitimate Arizona original — a tiny town (population: 6) on the Apache Trail that has operated since 1904. The saloon-style restaurant is covered floor-to-ceiling in dollar bills signed by visitors from all over the world. Order the chili, the prickly pear ice cream, or both. Cash preferred. This is the kind of place you can't make up.

5
2:00 – 4:00 PM · Return Drive

The Apache Trail Back — or the Long Way Around

You can return the way you came (fast, scenic) or continue on AZ-88 past Apache Lake and Roosevelt Lake for the full Apache Trail loop — but fair warning: the road past Tortilla Flat becomes unpaved and seriously rugged. A high-clearance vehicle is recommended. If you have it and want it, it's extraordinary. If not, the return on the paved section is still a great drive.

Why This Day Trip Works for Everyone

For tourists visiting Phoenix or Scottsdale: This is the outdoor Arizona experience that most visitors miss. Sedona gets all the attention, but it's 2+ hours away. Canyon Lake is 45 minutes and genuinely wild — the canyon walls, the bighorn sheep, the 100-year-old dam infrastructure, the total lack of cell service past Apache Junction. It feels like an expedition.

For locals who haven't been: A surprising number of Phoenix-area residents have never paddled Canyon Lake. It's close enough to feel spontaneous — leave at 8, be back by 3 with a full day of desert adventure on your record.

For groups: Canyon Lake handles groups well. We accommodate bachelorette parties, birthday groups, corporate teams, and family reunions on the water. If you're planning a group day trip from Scottsdale or Phoenix, reach out before you book so we can coordinate equipment and timing.

✅ Day Trip Checklist

Pack: Water (more than you think), sunscreen SPF 50+, hat, sunglasses, quick-dry clothes, snacks, camera
Wear: Quick-dry athletic wear or swimsuit — no cotton
Bring cash: Tortilla Flat is cash-preferred and worth it
Leave early: 8am departure from Scottsdale is ideal
Book ahead: Kayak rentals can sell out on busy weekends

What to Know Before You Go

  • No major stores on the route. Once you turn onto AZ-88, you're in the Tonto National Forest. Grab gas, water, and snacks in Apache Junction before you get on the trail.
  • Cell service disappears past Goldfield Ghost Town. Download offline maps if you need navigation after that point.
  • Summer heat is real. June through September temperatures can hit 105°F+ at Canyon Lake. Go early, stay hydrated, and you'll be fine — but don't leave your car without water.
  • The lake is open year-round except for the 2026 closure window (September 16, 2026 through approximately January 2027). Fall and spring are the most comfortable seasons for a day trip.
  • Free parking is available on site at Canyon Lake Marina — no day-use fee required.
