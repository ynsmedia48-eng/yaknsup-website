Canyon Lake Is Closing for 16 Weeks in 2026 — Here's Everything You Need to Know

By Yak N Sup · June 2026 · 6 min read · Canyon Lake, AZ

If you've been planning a trip to Canyon Lake this fall or winter, there's something important you need to know: Canyon Lake will be fully closed starting September 16, 2026 for approximately 16 weeks of critical dam safety inspections and maintenance.

This is a big deal. The closure is one of the most significant events in Canyon Lake's history — and it only happens once every 20 years. For paddlers, hikers, boaters, and anyone who loves this lake, here's the full breakdown of what's happening, why it's happening, and what it means for your plans.

⚠️ The Key Dates

Closure begins: September 16, 2026
Expected duration: ~16 weeks (approximately 4 months)
Expected reopening: Around January 2027
Last day to paddle with Yak N Sup: Late August 2026

Why Is Canyon Lake Closing?

SRP (Salt River Project) and the Bureau of Reclamation will be lowering Canyon Lake's water level by 54 feet to perform critical dam safety inspections on Mormon Flat Dam and Horse Mesa Dam — the structures that have held Canyon Lake in place since it was built in 1925.

Here's the thing: some of the most important equipment on those dams is permanently underwater. The only way to inspect and maintain it is to drain the lake down far enough to expose it. And that's not a quick process.

SRP performs these deep inspections every 20 years. The last one was around 2006. These aren't optional checkups — they're how you ensure a dam holding 57,000 acre-feet of water (enough to supply 173,000 Arizona families for a full year) stays safe and reliable for the next two decades.

How Long Will It Take?

  • 1

    Weeks 1–4: Drawdown

    The water level is gradually lowered by 54 feet. This takes approximately 4 weeks and fully exposes the underwater portions of Mormon Flat and Horse Mesa dams.

  • 2

    Weeks 5–12: Inspection & Maintenance

    SRP, the Bureau of Reclamation, and other agencies perform the actual inspection and maintenance work — 8 weeks focused on the dam structures, the river channel downstream, and any other canyon facilities that need attention while accessible.

  • 3

    Weeks 13–16: Refill

    Water is pumped back from upstream reservoirs. It takes another 4 weeks to bring the lake back to its normal level. No water is wasted in this process.

What Does This Mean for the Canyon Lake Area?

The Tonto National Forest will close the lake entirely — all boat ramps, access points, and shoreline areas — during the closure period. This is for public safety given the dramatic water level change and the active construction work happening on the dams.

That means no kayaking, no boating, no swimming, no hiking to the shoreline. Canyon Lake as we know it will essentially be inaccessible from mid-September 2026 through approximately January 2027.

📍 What about surrounding trails?

Check with the Tonto National Forest and SRP for official guidance on trail access during the drawdown. The Apache Trail (AZ-88) and some surrounding hiking areas may remain open, but anything that leads directly to the lake will be closed. Always verify before you go.

What About Yak N Sup?

We're fully open right now and will be operating through the summer and up to the closure in mid-September. That means you still have a solid window to get on the water before the lake goes down.

Honestly? This makes the summer of 2026 more special than usual. Canyon Lake is about to go through something that happens once a generation. Paddling it this summer — seeing it the way it's always been, full and alive — is something worth doing before it changes for a few months.

✅ Plan Your Visit Before It Closes

Yak N Sup is open 7 days a week, 8am–5:30pm at Canyon Lake Marina. Guided tours, full moon paddles, sunset paddles, and rentals are all available now. Book online to guarantee your spot — summer weekends fill up fast.

The Bigger Picture: Why This Matters

Canyon Lake stores over 57,000 acre-feet of water — enough to supply approximately 173,000 Arizona families for a full year. The Salt River Project has been managing this infrastructure for over 120 years, and the dam network (Saguaro, Canyon, Apache, Roosevelt) is what makes the Phoenix metro's water supply possible.

This closure isn't bad news — it's responsible stewardship. When Canyon Lake reopens in early 2027, those dams will have been inspected down to the foundation and certified safe for another 20 years. That's worth four months.
