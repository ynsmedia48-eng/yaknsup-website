La Barge and Tortilla Creek: Canyon Lake's Hidden Paddling Spots

By Yak N Sup·January 2025·6 min read·Canyon Lake, AZ

Most visitors to Canyon Lake see a wide, gorgeous body of water surrounded by canyon walls. They paddle the main lake, they're impressed, they go home. And they have no idea what they missed.

Because tucked into the canyon are two places that most people never find: La Barge Creek and Tortilla Creek — narrow, protected passages that branch off the main lake and go deeper into the Superstition Wilderness. You can't reach them by foot. There's no road. The only way in is by kayak or paddleboard.

If you've paddled Canyon Lake and you haven't been to La Barge or Tortilla Creek, you haven't seen Canyon Lake.

La Barge Creek

La Barge Creek enters Canyon Lake from the north, draining out of the Superstition Wilderness through a winding canyon passage. When water levels are normal, you can paddle a significant distance up the creek mouth before the passage becomes too shallow — and what you find in there is remarkable.

The canyon walls tighten as you go deeper. The noise from the main lake drops away. The water changes color — shallower sections reveal the bottom in clear detail, all sand and smooth rock. Herons stand on boulders and watch you pass. The canyon wraps around you and the sky becomes a narrow blue stripe above walls of orange and red.

What makes La Barge especially worth the paddle: it's one of the most reliable spots on the lake for bighorn sheep sightings. The canyon walls here are prime habitat — steep, rocky, and far from where hikers ever go. Early morning paddlers in La Barge consistently report sheep on the ledges above.

📍 How to Find La Barge Creek

La Barge Creek branches off the north side of the main lake. Our guides on Canyon Tours take guests through this passage — it's one of the highlights of the 2-hour guided experience. If you're going on your own, launch from Canyon Lake Marina and paddle north along the canyon wall. Look for where the shoreline opens into a narrow passage heading inland.

Tortilla Creek

Tortilla Creek comes in from the east, draining the canyon terrain on the far side of the lake from the marina. It's slightly more remote — a longer paddle to reach — but that distance is part of what makes it special. By the time you reach the Tortilla Creek mouth, you're well away from the boat traffic and noise of the main lake.

The creek mouth area features some of the best examples of desert varnish on Canyon Lake — those dark vertical streaks on the canyon walls created by mineral deposits and microbial mats over thousands of years. The contrast of black varnish against orange sandstone is striking, and the reflections in the still water of the creek make it one of the most photogenic spots on the whole lake.

Water clarity in Tortilla Creek tends to be exceptional. The shallow sections near the creek mouth are crystal clear on calm days — you can see every rock on the bottom, and sometimes fish hanging in the current.

Best Conditions for Exploring the Creeks

Both passages are best explored when the lake is at a normal or higher water level. In drought years when the lake drops significantly, the creek mouths can become too shallow to paddle very far. Check the SRP lake level page before your visit if exploring the creeks is a priority.

  • Morning is best — calmer main lake to cross, better wildlife activity, and the morning light on the canyon walls inside the creeks is extraordinary
  • Calm wind days — when wind picks up on the main lake, the crossing can get choppy. The creeks themselves are sheltered, but getting there takes a crossing
  • Two hours minimum — if you want to explore La Barge or Tortilla Creek properly, book a 2-hour rental or consider a Canyon Tour where your guide takes you there
  • Kayak or SUP only — the passages are not accessible by motorized craft

What to Look For

When you're paddling through these creek passages, here's what to watch for:

  • Great blue herons — almost always present in the creek mouths, standing on rocks
  • Bighorn sheep — scan the upper canyon walls, especially in La Barge Creek early morning
  • Desert varnish — the dark streaks on canyon walls, visible everywhere in Tortilla Creek especially
  • Water color changes — where the creek water meets the main lake, you'll sometimes see a visible color difference, especially after rain
  • Cave entrances — the Superstition Mountains have many shallow caves and alcoves visible from the water level in these passages
