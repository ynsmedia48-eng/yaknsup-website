What to Bring to Canyon Lake (The Complete Packing List)

By Yak N Sup · June 2026 · 5 min read · Canyon Lake, AZ

The number one thing we hear from first-timers after a rental? "I wish I had brought more water." The second most common? "I forgot sunscreen."

Canyon Lake is stunning. It's also 45 minutes from the nearest store, sitting inside the Tonto National Forest, fully exposed to the Arizona sun. A great day on the water starts with packing right — and this list is exactly what our crew would tell you to bring.

⚠️ The #1 Mistake First-Timers Make

Not bringing enough to eat and drink. The desert sun is deceptively intense, you're working your arms and core the whole time, and the drive back to Tempe or Phoenix is long. Pack more than you think you need.

The Non-Negotiables

  • Water — lots of it. The rule of thumb: at least 12 oz per person per hour you plan to be on the water. In summer (May–September), double that. A standard water bottle isn't enough for a 2-hour rental in July. Bring a full liter per person, minimum. Hydration packs are ideal for guided tours.
  • Sunscreen — SPF 50+ and reef-safe. Apply before you leave the car, not after you launch. The water reflects UV rays back at you, which means you're getting hit from above AND below. Reapply every 90 minutes. Don't forget the back of your neck, the tops of your feet, and the back of your knees.
  • A wide-brim hat. The sun at Canyon Lake is relentless from about 9am to 4pm. A hat that actually shades your face, ears, and neck makes the difference between a great day and a sunburned one. Baseball caps leave your ears and neck exposed.
  • Sunglasses — polarized preferred. The glare off the water is intense. Polarized lenses cut through the reflection and let you actually see into the water — which is especially nice when you're paddling through the cove sections where the canyon walls go straight into the lake.
  • Snacks. Paddling is exercise — you'll be hungrier than you expect. Easy, portable options that travel well: trail mix, protein bars, fruit, nuts. Avoid anything that melts (chocolate, granola with yogurt chips) in summer heat. Stay away from heavy meals before paddling; you'll feel it.

What to Wear

The golden rule: no cotton. Cotton absorbs water, gets cold and heavy when wet, and dries slowly. One splash and you're uncomfortable for the rest of the rental. Wear quick-dry athletic wear or swimwear instead.

  • Quick-dry shorts or athletic bottoms. Board shorts, athletic shorts, leggings with UPF fabric — all great. Avoid jeans, khakis, or anything thick that holds water.
  • A moisture-wicking or UPF shirt. In summer, a lightweight long-sleeve UPF shirt is actually cooler than bare skin — it blocks the sun, keeps you cooler, and means less sunscreen to reapply. In cooler months (October–April), bring a light layer you can tie around your waist.
  • Water shoes or sandals with a strap. You may need to step out of the kayak or off the board onto rocks or the marina dock. Flip-flops slip off in water. Closed-toe water shoes or sandals with a back strap (like Chacos or Tevas) are ideal. Bare feet work too, but watch for hot surfaces in summer.
💡 What About Swimwear?

Swimsuits are perfect for summer rentals. If you plan to get in the water (swimming is allowed at certain areas of the lake), wear your swimsuit under your quick-dry clothes. Life jackets are worn on top regardless.

Smart Extras to Bring

  • A dry bag (or rent one from us for $10). Your phone, car keys, ID, and snacks will thank you. Even if you don't plan to flip, spray happens. A 5–10L dry bag is enough for most people. Ask us at check-in if you need to rent one.
  • A small cooler bag or insulated tote. If you're bringing lunch or cold drinks (especially in summer), a soft-sided cooler keeps things cold and compact. Hard coolers are awkward in a kayak cockpit.
  • Bug spray. Mornings and evenings near the water can bring out gnats and mosquitoes, especially in spring. If you're doing an early tour or a sunset paddle, bring bug spray.
  • A waterproof phone case or mount. If you want photos on the water, a waterproof pouch that floats is better than just hoping your phone doesn't fall in. There are bighorn sheep on those canyon walls — you'll want your camera.
  • Towels and a change of clothes for the car. Even if you don't flip, you'll likely get wet from paddle drip. Leave a dry towel and change of clothes in the car for after. The drive back to Phoenix in wet clothes is not comfortable.

What Yak N Sup Provides

  • Kayak or paddleboard (single or tandem)

✅ Quick Reference: What to Bring

Must-haves: Water (1+ liter/person), sunscreen (SPF 50+), hat, sunglasses, snacks, quick-dry clothing, water shoes
Smart extras: Dry bag, soft cooler, bug spray, waterproof phone case, towel + change of clothes in the car
We provide: Kayak/SUP, paddle, life jacket, safety briefing, dry bag rentals ($10)
